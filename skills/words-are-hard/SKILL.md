# Humanizer

A style filter for prose. Keep the meaning, facts, and the user's voice intact. Change how it's said, not what's said.

## Process

1. Write the draft.
2. Check it against every section below: words, constructions, structure, formatting.
3. Fix violations silently. Don't announce the fixes.
4. Reread as if out loud. If it sounds like a press release, brochure, or LinkedIn influencer, rewrite it.

If the user asks for a rewrite of existing text, end with one line listing the habits removed. Otherwise just deliver the text.

## Banned words

Use the plain word or restructure the sentence.

delve, pivotal, crucial, vital, significant (as filler), tapestry, landscape (abstract), interplay, intricate, intricacies, meticulous, vibrant, robust, enduring, testament, underscore, highlight (verb), showcase, emphasize, enhance, foster, garner, bolster, align with, boasts, additionally, furthermore, moreover, deep dive, valuable insights, key (vague adjective), in the heart of, nestled, renowned, groundbreaking, profound, rich (filler), diverse array, commitment to, seamless, leverage (verb), elevate, unlock, empower, journey (metaphor), realm, navigate (metaphor), game-changer, cutting-edge, ever-evolving.

## Banned constructions

- **Em dashes.** None. Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses.
- **Inflated significance.** No "marks a pivotal moment," "stands as a testament," "reflects a broader trend," "setting the stage for." Don't puff a small thing into a turning point.
- **Trailing -ing clauses.** No ", highlighting...", ", ensuring...", ", reflecting..." tacked on to sound deep.
- **Copula avoidance.** Say "is" and "has." Not "serves as," "stands as," "functions as," "features," "boasts," "offers."
- **Negative parallelism.** No "it's not just X, it's Y," "not only... but also," "this isn't about X, it's about Y."
- **Forced triples.** Don't group adjectives or phrases in threes by default.
- **Vague attribution.** No "experts say," "studies show," "many believe" without a real named source.
- **Challenges boilerplate.** No "Despite these challenges..." or "while there's still work to be done."
- **Rhetorical setups.** No "The result?", "Here's the thing:", "Let that sink in.", "And honestly?"
- **Chatbot artifacts.** No "Certainly!", "Great question," "I hope this helps," "Let me know if...", "Here's a..." openers, cutoff disclaimers.
- **Wrap-ups.** No "In summary," "In conclusion," "Overall," "Ultimately," or a final sentence restating the point.

## Structural tells

These matter more than word choice. Check the whole piece, not just sentences.

- **Uniform rhythm.** If paragraphs are all the same length, or sentences all land at 15 to 20 words, break it up. Some one-line paragraphs. Some fragments.
- **Symmetry.** Real writing is lopsided. Don't give every point equal weight or equal length.
- **Over-hedging.** One honest hedge is fine. Hedging every claim is not.
- **Neat bow endings.** End when the point is made. No moral, no call to reflection, no inspirational closer.
- **Topic-sentence-then-elaborate on every paragraph.** Vary it. Lead with a detail, a number, or the conclusion sometimes.
- **Generic sentences.** Cut any sentence that could appear unchanged in a piece about a different topic.
- **Balanced to a fault.** If the user has a view, state it. Don't invent a counterpoint for balance.

## Formatting tells

- No bold scattered through prose.
- No emoji on headings or bullets.
- Sentence case headings, not Title Case.
- No bullets for things that should be a paragraph. Social posts and emails are prose.
- No one-sentence-per-line LinkedIn staircase unless the user asks for it.

## Write like this instead

- Terse. Say it once, plainly. Cut throat-clearing.
- Contractions. Plain words: "use" not "utilize," "has" not "features," "help" not "facilitate."
- Specifics: real names, numbers, dates, places, the actual thing.
- A point of view. First person where a person would use it.
- Uneven rhythm. Short sentences next to longer ones.
- Match the medium: a text is shorter and looser than an email; a caption is shorter than both.

## Don't overcorrect

- Don't strip all personality to avoid tells. Flat isn't human either.
- Don't add fake typos, slang, or forced casualness.
- A banned word is fine when it's the literal correct term (a "robust" statistical method, a "landscape" painting). Use judgment, not find-and-replace.
